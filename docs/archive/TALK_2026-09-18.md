# Talk notes, OCT to MRI registration (Sep 18)

*About 3.5 minutes. Screen: the 9.18 doc, scroll along.*

Quick update. I've implemented the registration method as we designed it, and it now registers our I58 brainstem pair fully automatically. Checked by eye, the brainstem body lines up to within a few tenths of a millimeter. So we now have a working base, and from here we build on it together.

**[Method]**

The input is an OCT block embedded in agarose, and an MRI that's already cropped around it. There are five steps.

Step one is the specimen mask. In the OCT, agarose is as bright as tissue, so a threshold doesn't work. But the agarose artifacts are section stripes and tile seams, and each of them changes along only one axis. Tissue has texture in every direction, so that's what the mask keeps.

Step two is the score. Both volumes get turned into a rough bright and dark map, and we compare those, plus the block outline. On the outline, we only penalize OCT tissue sitting on MRI background, since the crop can have extra tissue around the block. One nice property here. If you swap bright and dark in the OCT, the map part of the score flips sign, so the sign tells us if the contrast is inverted.

Step three is the search. The MRI is already cropped, so we don't need a whole brain search. We go through eight thousand rotations, and for each one, FFTs give us the score at every shift in the crop. Left and right come from the file headers, so no mirror images.

Step four takes the best 24 poses and fits a full affine, with a penalty on scale and shear. Without that penalty, the fit would just shrink the block and hide it inside the MRI tissue.

Step five is the fine refinement. The coarse score can't settle the last few millimeters, so we refine using gradient directions inside both volumes. These follow fiber bundles and vessels, and they don't need the OCT and MRI intensities to match. On I58 this step moves the block by about 2.4 millimeters.

The new parts are the texture mask and this one score. The last step uses a known measure, normalized gradient fields.

**[Result, the figure]**

This is I58, one plane per MRI axis, with a checkerboard on the right. The run takes about 11 minutes on one GPU, with no manual input. Follow the vessels and fiber bundle edges across the checkerboard, and they carry straight through.

Each piece matters. With a plain threshold instead of the texture mask, without the outline, or without the scale penalty, the block lands about forty millimeters off.

Two regions don't fit. The sections at one end of the block are ten to fourteen percent larger than in the MRI, which puts them up to two millimeters off. And a few torn pieces of cerebellum moved during sectioning. Both are the tissue deforming, not a wrong pose.

---

*Does it work on the public DANDI data?* Not well. The method leans on the agarose outline. The DANDI blocks are cortex slabs cut without agarose, so the outline says very little, and only one of the five blocks with a known position registered.

*Why do the file headers decide left and right?* On a nearly symmetric block like I58, the coarse score can't tell a pose from its mirror image, and the mirrored pose sits about 30 millimeters off. The headers settle it. If a file has no header, we run both sides and the gradient step picks, because fine structure doesn't match in a mirror image.

*How accurate is it without labels?* Right now it's visual, plane by plane on the overlays. That's why the landmarks are next, to put a number on it.

*Why not a non-rigid registration?* The brainstem body already fits with an affine. The error that's left is mostly at one end of the block, so a correction along the section axis is the natural next step. The torn cerebellum pieces are a separate issue.
